Guide & shortcuts

Draw bar — tools

Select — tap/click an object's outline to select it (not its interior). Tap another to add it; tap a selected one again to drop it. Drag a selected object to move it, drag a white handle to reshape it, or drag empty paper to sweep a selection box around several objects.

Erase — tap/click any line, shape, or measurement's outline to delete it immediately, one tap per object.

Line — click each point in turn. Return, a double-tap on the last point, or the on-screen Finish button ends it as an open line; ⇧Return or Close shape ends it as a closed polygon instead.

Rect — click one corner, then the opposite corner.

Circle — click the centre, then a point on the edge to set the radius.

Arc — click the centre, then the start point, then move the pointer to sweep the arc and click to finish. Once selected, drag either end to extend the arc from that side (the far end stays put), or the round handle at its midpoint to resize the radius — nothing here rotates the arc; use Rotate/Flip for that.

Half — click the centre, then a point on the edge, then a third click on whichever side the half-circle should open toward. Editing handles behave the same as Arc above.

Measure — click two points; a dashed dimension line and length label appear between them. Drag its round handle to offset the label away from the geometry. Click a third point instead of finishing and it becomes an angle measurement between the two rays at that middle point — handy for a triangle's corner angle. Tapping directly on an existing arc measures its sweep angle immediately, without clicking three points by hand.

Sketch — press and drag a rough gesture (a real drag, not a tap) — a line, circle, rectangle, or curve — and release; it's replaced by a clean, precise version of whatever it recognizes.

Pan — drag anywhere to move the view. (Space+drag or two-finger drag/scroll work from any tool without switching to this one.)

Snap / Ortho / Polar — toggles. Snap turns the whole snapping engine on or off. Ortho locks new segments to 0°/90° from the last point. Polar locks to the increment set in Settings (15° by default).

Edit bar — actions

Type value — opens exact numeric entry for whatever point the active tool needs next (coordinates, length + angle, radius, or sweep), instead of clicking it.

Finish / Close shape / Cancel — end the current multi-point line as open or closed, or abandon it and discard the points placed so far.

Undo / Redo — one step at a time; a whole drag, reshape, or style change counts as a single step regardless of how far you moved it.

Delete / Select all — remove or select every canSelect (unlocked, visible) object. Delete, Copy, Group, and Ungroup all act on whatever's currently selected, regardless of which drawing tool is active — a selection isn't lost just by switching tools to look at something else. If nothing is selected at all, clicking one of these switches you to Select automatically so you can pick something.

Copy / Paste — duplicates the current selection; each paste offsets a little further than the last so repeats don't stack exactly on top of each other.

Group / Ungroup — fuses 2+ selected objects into one unit that selects, moves, and styles as a single thing; Ungroup restores the originals. Merge makes a group permanent — it behaves exactly like a group in every other way, but Ungroup will no longer take it apart.

Fit — reframes the view to show everything you've drawn.

Import an SVG — in the Open menu. The drawing arrives as ordinary canSelect shapes: curves become polylines, so every point can be dragged, and a rectangle or circle that maps cleanly stays one. You are asked for a width on import because there is no scale tool afterwards, then asked whether to keep it in your symbol set — say yes and it is available from Symbols from then on, until you delete it there. You can also add anything you have drawn: select it, open Symbols, name it, tap Add. Anything the importer could not use is recorded in Settings → About → View log.

Full screen — hides both toolbars and the status strip so the sheet takes the whole window; an "Exit full screen" button appears at the top right. Where the browser supports it, this also drops the browser's own chrome. The setting is remembered. Note this is the app's own control: if you have previously seen a similar expand button drawn outside the app, that belonged to whatever was displaying the page, and is not present when this file is opened directly or from a Home Screen icon.

Save / Open — each opens one menu listing every option for that verb (file, SVG/PNG, print, text, browser storage) — see "Save menu" and "Open menu" below.

Keys — opens this panel. Settings — opens the Sheet settings panel.

When something is selected

A floating bar appears at the bottom of the canvas: Style (color/thickness/dash), Rotate/Flip, Delete, Layer ▾ (opens a picker of every layer, plus a "+ New" field to create one on the spot — tap or create-and-tap to move the selection straight onto it), Split at intersections (see below), and Deselect.

Split at intersections — select a line or a rectangle, tap this, and it breaks into separate pieces everywhere it crosses other geometry (other lines, a circle, another rectangle's edges). Untouched corners stay inside whichever piece they belong to — each piece runs from one crossing point to the next, not from every original corner. Each resulting piece is then independently canSelect and deletable — useful for turning a cross of two lines into an L-shape by deleting just one arm, or opening a gap in a rectangle where something else cuts across it.

Style popover — a color swatch row (plus a custom color picker) for the outline, a matching row for Fill (only visible on closed shapes — rectangles, circles, and polygons closed back to their start; harmless and invisible on open lines), a thickness slider, and four dash styles. Applies to every selected object at once, recursively into any group.

Rotate/Flip popover — quick angle buttons (±45°/±90°/180°), a custom-degree field, and Flip Horizontal/Vertical. Rotates or mirrors around the selection's own center — one object around itself, several together around their shared center, or check "each around its own center" to spin multiple objects in place independently instead.

Save menu

Save to file (.json, reopen later with Open) · Export as SVG · Export as PNG · Print at actual size (set the print dialog to 100%, not "fit to page") · Copy as text (no file at all — shows the drawing as text you copy). Below a divider, Save in this browser gives it a name and keeps it locally, separate from any of the above.

Open menu

Open a file (.json from this tool) · Paste from text (the counterpart to Copy as text). Below a divider, a list of anything saved in this browser, each with its own Open and Delete.

Settings panel

Units & grid — the sheet's unit, X/Y interval (independently if "same on both axes" is off), and how often a heavy line appears. Layers — add, rename by tapping, and toggle visibility/lock/guide-status per layer, or delete one. Snapping — enable/disable each snap type individually. Angle constraint — Ortho/Polar defaults and the polar increment. Display — grid visibility, delta labels on measurements, area/perimeter labels, single-segment line mode, and whether two-finger trackpad scroll pans or zooms. Save & open — shortcuts to the same two menus above. Sheet — clear the whole drawing.

The status bar's Layer cell is also a button — tap it for a quick popover to switch the current layer without opening Settings at all.

Shortcuts, icons and visibility

Each row: show or hide the button, its icon, its colour, its key. Hiding removes the button only — the shortcut still works, so a bar can be trimmed to a job without losing anything. Saved with your workspace, and carried by a template if you have that switched on.

Bindings are saved with the drawing, and kept in this browser where browser storage is available.

iPad — finger or pencil

One finger draws, picks and drags. Two fingers pan and pinch to zoom, and never draw. With Select active, dragging an object moves it, dragging a white square handle reshapes it, and dragging empty paper sweeps a selection box.

Tap objects to add them to the selection and tap again to drop them. The red Delete bar appears at the bottom whenever something is selected. Erase deletes on a single tap.

iPad — with a keyboard

Every shortcut below works. Use ⌘ where a laptop uses Ctrl. Arrow keys nudge the selection by one grid square.

MacBook — trackpad

Click and drag behaves exactly like one finger on the iPad. Two-finger scroll pans the sheet and pinch zooms toward the pointer. Hold Space and drag to pan from anywhere, or use the Pan tool.
